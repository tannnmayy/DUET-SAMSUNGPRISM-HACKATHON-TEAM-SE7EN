The problem

Voice assistants take turns. People don't.

People interrupt, correct themselves and change their minds while they speak: "Set an alarm for 6:30… no, make it 7." Most voice assistants wait for silence, then act on everything that was said. Once an assistant can act (set alarms, send texts, run the home) that habit becomes dangerous: a setting changed on words the user took back, a message sent twice, "done" said before anything happened.

Theme 05 asks for an agent that stays responsive while it works, never claims a false "done", works asynchronously, and recovers cleanly: it throws away stale intent, updates an action's values after a change of mind, and never repeats a state-changing action. Round 1 measured this on Full-Duplex-Bench v3, 100 real recordings with corrections and 12 tools. For Round 2 the jury asked for a real use case on a real phone, and judges five things: the problem and its caveats, the core architecture and modules, the extension and its relevance to Samsung, how it scales to new use cases, and a live demo of all of it.

What we built

An Android app, a coordinator, and two minds

DUET for Galaxy is an Android app (the screen, the microphone and loudspeaker, and every phone action) connected over LiveKit to an agent on our laptop. The agent hears with Silero VAD and Whisper, speaks with Kokoro, and thinks with two Gemma 4 models: a small one on our GPU for simple actions, the large one in Google's cloud for everything else. One coordinator stands between both minds and the world. The home is a real SmartThings account.

Assistant

Alarms you can correct, battery help from the phone's real readings that you can interrupt, settings, apps, Maps, calls and texts, the torch, music, volume, the calendar, the weather, web answers, the SmartThings home.

Care

For an older person living alone: medicines with a double-dose guard, reminders, calls and texts to family, a daily check-in, a family alert, larger text.

Drive

Eyes on the road: a destination changed mid-sentence, the route handed to Google Maps, then the home, music and messages while Maps stays in front, and DUET speaking first as you near home.

One request, start to finish

  1. HearThe ears close the turn after 0.5 s of quiet and transcribe it.
  2. RouteThe fast mind looks first; a plain reading goes straight to its reading; an answer to DUET's own offer ("yes, make it 22") is settled from the offer, with no model.
  3. CheckCode checks the plan: named in the user's words, values they said, the last value said, not a question, nothing dropped.
  4. HoldThe commit gate holds the action until the user has been quiet; newer words void it.
  5. Act onceThe ledger lets it run exactly once, on the phone or the home.
  6. VerifyThe device reports back; a setting is read back.
  7. Say"Sure. Torch on." from the device's reply; the cloud mind adds only what is new.

Where each module runs, chosen by measured latency

We measured moving the AI onto the phone. Gemma 3n E2B on the Galaxy S24 Ultra's GPU runs at about 816 tokens a second of prompt and 16 of output (Google's published LiteRT-LM figures): our fast mind's 1,400-token prompt would take about 3.3 s there, against 0.24 s on our GPU. Speech recognition on the phone would gain at most 0.1–0.3 s and lose accuracy on Indian English. The network costs 90 ms. So the minds stay on the edge machine today; the phone does everything that is the phone's.

Extension and relevance to Samsung

The layer that makes an acting assistant safe to interrupt

One UI 8.5 turned Bixby into a conversational device agent that changes settings from plain speech. The moment an assistant acts, interruption and correction become a safety problem. DUET's coordinator is that safety layer, and it is model-independent: it could sit under Bixby's device agent and in front of every AppFunction call.

SmartThings, for real

DUET controls the account's devices and says "done" only once the device reports the new state. Devices are understood by their capabilities, so a new plug or light works without code.

DUET speaks first new

Nearly home, it reads the home and the outside temperature: "You'll be home in about 10 minutes, and it's 31 degrees outside. Shall I start the living room AC at 24 degrees?" (after dark, a light too). Accept it, change one part ("yes, make it 22": the rest is done as offered), take a part ("just the AC"), decline it, or talk over it. Samsung's Car-to-Home does this as a silent routine; DUET asks, explains and can be corrected.

The phone's assistant new

DUET answers Android's assist intent: chosen as the digital assistant, the assist gesture or side key opens it straight into a conversation (5 s from a cold start).

Galaxy actions on public APIs

No root, no hidden APIs: everything a Samsung app team could ship. Permissions are set over USB for the demo; Samsung's own apps already hold them.

The on-device path

The coordinator is plain code that ports to Kotlin; Gemma 4 E2B already runs on Galaxy phones (Gemma 4 E2B on the S26 Ultra: 3,808 prompt and 52 output tokens a second). When the phone's numbers beat the edge machine's, the minds move; the coordinator does not change.

A PRISM worklet

Next steps fit a 4–6 month worklet: the Kotlin coordinator, AppFunctions as an executor when privileged, physical SmartThings devices, Hindi and Tamil.

Scalability

A new use case is one entry in a file

A phone capability is declared in app/www/skills.json: its modes, whether it reads, changes or opens a screen, whether it needs consent, the words that name it, its exactly-once group, what DUET says when it is done, and how it runs (an Android setting written and read back, an intent, Do Not Disturb). It inherits the hold, the ledger, the guards, consent and confirmed-when-done with no code. The agent and the app read the file at each conversation start, so a new skill needs no new app and no restart. The format follows Android 16's AppFunctions, which a privileged agent could plug in as one more kind of executor.

{
  "name": "set_touch_vibration",
  "modes": ["assistant"],
  "kind": "write",
  "named": "vibrat|haptic",
  "group": "touch_vibration",
  "description": "Turn touch vibration on or off.",
  "parameters": {"on": {"type": "boolean"}},
  "required": ["on"],
  "confirm": "Touch vibration is {on?on:off}.",
  "run": {"type": "setting", "namespace": "system",
          "key": "haptic_feedback_enabled",
          "value": "{on?1:0}"},
  "verify": true
}
  1. +
    The entry is added to the laptop's skills.jsonno new APK, no restart
  2. ↓
    A new conversation: "Turn off touch vibration."
  3. ⚡
    Fast mind: set_touch_vibration(on: false)the entry's words named it, so it may act
  4. ✓
    Done and read back: haptic_feedback_enabled = 00.86 s; confirmed with adb
  5. 💬
    "Touch vibration is off."

Shipped skills: auto-rotate, text size, Do Not Disturb, the camera, the Wi-Fi panel. The same idea runs through the rest: SmartThings devices by capability, modes as configuration (tools, instructions, greeting), and the coordinator unchanged for all of them.

Showing how DUET recovers

The Drills panel (under each mode) makes a failure happen on demand, and the timeline shows the recovery: cut the cloud mind (said within 2 s; the torch still works), slow it by 6 s ("One moment… Still working on it", simple actions unaffected), make the phone refuse actions (DUET says what to allow and claims nothing), cut SmartThings, or drop the connection (back in about 4 s).

Known limits and next steps

What it is today, and where it goes

Today

An Android app plus our edge machine (the laptop's GPU) and the cloud; it needs internet. The free Gemma tier allows about six cloud questions a minute. Care's medicines and Drive's car are simulated and labelled. SmartThings tokens last 24 hours and allow about 10 requests a minute, so DUET reads the home sparingly: a command's own reply updates the panel, and a short limit is waited out.

Only the S24 can answer

Loudspeaker echo (fallback: Galaxy Buds), Samsung's background rules, which volume DUET's voice uses, and the side-key setting. All are on the checklist for the night of the 20th.

Next

The coordinator in Kotlin on the phone; Gemma 4 E2B on the phone when its numbers beat the edge machine's; AppFunctions as an executor; physical devices; Hindi and Tamil; guided troubleshooting of the phone.
