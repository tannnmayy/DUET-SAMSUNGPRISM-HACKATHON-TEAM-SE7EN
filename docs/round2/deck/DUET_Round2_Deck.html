    2Brief point 2 · Core technology module

    The coordinator: the model proposes, code decides

    Epochs

    Every plan carries the epoch of the words it was made on. New words void it, even after the newer turn has closed. A cough brings no words, so it only delays.

    Commit gate

    No action while you speak. Thinking starts at once; acting waits until you have been quiet for:

    1.4 s1.8 s after “no wait”, “make it”2.2 s after “and…”

    Idempotency ledger

    The same call is answered from the ledger, never sent twice. A call past the gate finishes and is recorded even if you barge in.

    Failure policy

    A failed read is retried once. A failed or timed-out write is never re-sent: DUET says it may not have gone through.

    2Brief point 2 · Semantics and acoustics

    It hears what you say and how you say it

    Tone may make DUET wait or ask. It never runs or cancels an action on its own: every action still needs words.

    Unsure

    built

    “Set an alarm for 4:30, I think.”

    Asks “4:30 in the afternoon, right?” before acting. “Yes” then sets it, with no model call.

    words: a hedge next to the value · voice: a long pause before it

    Urgent

    built

    “No, no, no!” shouted over an answer

    Stops at once and stays quiet. Pending actions wait for the words before anything runs.

    voice: brighter +13 dB, higher +9 semitones · words: “no”

    Distressed

    built

    “My knee hurts…” in a low, shaky voice

    Answers gently, asks how they are, offers to call family, and speaks 10% slower.

    voice: emotion model · words: pain

    Mixed signals

    next

    “I'm fine” said shakily

    The words and the voice disagree: check in gently and offer to alert family.

    the voice must outweigh the words, so it needs care

    Sarcasm

    next

    “Oh great, set it for 5 AM.”

    At most a quick yes/no check before acting. Never acted on.

    a research-grade cue, so advisory only

    Why brightness and pitch, not loudness: a phone's gain control flattens loudness. On CREMA-D actors, angry speech was only +2.3 dB louder after levelling, but +18.9 dB brighter and +14 semitones higher. Each segment is measured in about 12 ms, in parallel with speech recognition.

    A1 · Latency

    Where the time goes, and why the AI runs on the edge today

    A simple action, seconds after your last word

    Medians from our runs. Thinking and the hold overlap: the hold is counted from the end of speech, so safety costs little time. A correction (“no, make it 7”) raises the hold to 1.8 s; a cloud answer adds one Gemma 4 26B call, 1.5–2 s at the median.

    A2 · The fast mind

    How a 2-billion-parameter model is kept safe

    What the fast mind may do
    • Answer small talk itself (“Good morning”, “I'm tired”), after a code check: no number, no health advice, no claim, no tool name. 0.25–0.45 s.
    • Act on a simple phone action: alarm, timer, torch, brightness, a call, a text, Maps, a home device.
    • Keep listening when the words stop mid-phrase (“Call my…”).
    • Hand over with a bridge line in its own words and a kind: web → one grounded search · health or talk → one light call · phone → the full cloud mind with tools.
    • Plain readings (“How much battery do I have?”) are answered from the reading itself, with no model call.
    Code checks every plan before it runs
    • Named in your words: “how many shopping apps do I have?” is not open_app(apps).
    • Values you said: a contact, place or number you never said goes to the cloud mind.
    • The last value said is in the plan: “3:30, no, cancel that, 4:30 instead” must plan 4:30.
    • A question is answered, never acted on: “Is the AC on?”
    • Nothing dropped, nothing earlier changed: “…actually never mind” and “make the alarm 7” go to the cloud mind, which has the history.

    Measured: 188 of 190 decisions right, 0 wrong, 2 handed over; actions decided in 0.18–0.24 s. A plan that fails a check costs a second, never a wrong action.

    Each take against the same actor's neutral one. Plain speech marked raised: 0 of 13 of our own S24 utterances, 0 of 40 synthetic lines. Excitement sounds like anger, so a raised voice alone changes nothing but the timeline.

    Models and cues we tried
    • SenseVoice-Small: “unknown” emotion on 49 of 50 English clips. Rejected.
    • emotion2vec, distilled to 9.7 MB: 19 of 30 negative clips caught, 1 false alarm in 20. Used only together with distress words.
    • A rising pitch at the end: flagged 6 of 29 plain statements; Indian English often ends rising. Off by default.
    • Our own pitch tracker made octave errors, so we use Praat.
    • Cost: 12 ms a segment at the median, 31 ms at most, in parallel with Whisper.
    A4 · Testing

    Tested like a product, including failures on purpose

    The layers
    • 345 unit tests: the gate, epochs, ledger, guards, routing, key pool and tone.
